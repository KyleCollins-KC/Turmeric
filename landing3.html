Sample preparation for NMR

Scroll down to see how all the samples were prepared ready for NMR analysis

  • metanil yellow preparation
  • UV-Vis sample preparation

Step 1

Approximately 10 mg of each turmeric sample was weighed using a Mettler Toledo AB104 analytical balance into a green 2 mL Eppendorf Safe-Lock tube.

Figure 1: Video demonstration of turmeric being weighed for sample preparation

Step 2

550 µL of d-DMSO (99.9% atom deuterium with 0.03% (v/v) TMS, 0.6 mL from Sigma-Aldrich) was added to the Eppendorf tubes using a 20–200 µL Eppendorf Research pipette. Except TU009, where the powder was instead washed from the surface of the finger with approximately 600 µL of d-DMSO

Figure 2: video demostration of d-DMSO being added to the Eppendorf tubes

Step 3

The samples were mixed for 60 seconds at 2000 RPM using Fisher Scientific FB15012 TopMix This step was necessary because static interactions caused the turmeric to cling to the walls of the Eppendorf tubes, and mixing ensured full contact between the sample and the solvent

Figure 3: video demostration of the samples being mixed

Step 4

The samples were then ultrasonicated using a Fisher Scientific FB15046 ultrasonic bath for 15 minutes at room temperature. Which maximise the solubility of compounds that are soluble under these conditions

Figure 4: video demostration of the ultrasonic treamtment the samples received

Step 5

Following this, the samples were centrifuged for 10 minutes at 14,000 RPM and 20°C using a Beckman Allegra X-22R Benchtop Centrifuge

Figure 5: video demostration of the samples being centrifuged

Step 6

The supernatant was then carefully collected to avoid disturbing the solid at the bottom using a 20–200 µL Eppendorf Research pipette

Figure 6: video demostration of the supernatant being collected

Step 7

The collected supernatant was then transferred into 5 mm NMR tubes from Wilmad® for NMR analysis. where the samples were analysed using a Bruker 500 MHz NMR spectrometer at 300 K. Chemical shifts (δ) were reported in ppm relative to the residual d-DMSO proton signal (δ = 2.50 ppm). The spectra were acquired with 16 scans, an acquisition time of 3 seconds, and a relaxation delay of 1 second.

 

Metanil yellow spiked sample NMR prepration

Approximately 5 mg of TU002 and metanil yellow was added to a 2 mL green Eppendorf Safe-Lock tube. Then steps 2 though 7 for the NMR preparation were followed as described above.

 

Sample preparation for UV-Vis

Scroll down to see how all the samples were prepared for UV-Vis analysis using DMSO as the solvent

10 µL was taken from the NMR tubes of the corresponding samples using a 2–20 µL Starlab pipette and diluted with 990 µL of DMSO (≥99.9% purity) from Sigma-Aldrich. Further dilutions were prepared for curcumin (100 µL in 900 µL DMSO) and metanil yellow (200 µL in 800 µL DMSO). The 100 and 200 µL transfers were made using a 20–200 µL Eppendorf Research pipette, while the 800 and 900 µL transfers were performed using a 100–1000 µL Starlab pipette. The UV-Vis data was collected using either the Hitachi U-3310 or Hitachi U-3900 spectrophotometer operating in absorbance mode, with a scan speed of 300 nm/min and a slit width of 2 nm. Spectra were recorded across different wavelength ranges, with the maximum range spanning 190–800 nm. Quartz cuvettes with a 1 cm path length were used for all measurements, and baseline correction was performed using DMSO blank.
